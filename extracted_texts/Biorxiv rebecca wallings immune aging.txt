26 Abstract: Considering age is the greatest risk factor for many neurodegenerative diseases, aging, in
27 particular aging of the immune system, is the most underappreciated and understudied contributing factor
28 in the neurodegeneration field. Genetic variation around the LRRK2 gene affects risk of both familial and
29 sporadic Parkinson's disease (PD). The leucine-rich repeat kinase 2 (LRRK2) protein has been implicated
30 in peripheral immune signaling, however, the effects of an aging immune system on LRRK2 function have
31 been neglected to be considered. We demonstrate here that the R1441C mutation induces a hyper-
32 responsive phenotype in macrophages from young female mice, characterized by increased effector
33 functions, including stimulation-dependent antigen presentation, cytokine release, phagocytosis, and
34 lysosomal function. This is followed by age-acquired immune cell exhaustion in a Lrrk2-kinase-dependent
35 manner. Immune-exhausted macrophages exhibit suppressed antigen presentation and hypophagocytosis,
36 which is also demonstrated in myeloid cells from R1441C and Y1699C-PD patients. Our novel findings
37 that LRRK2 mutations confer immunological advantage at a young age but may predispose the carrier to
38 age-acquired immune cell exhaustion have significant implications for LRRK2 biology and therapeutic
39 development. Indeed, LRRK2 has become an appealing target in PD, but our findings suggest that more
40 research is required to understand the cell-type specific consequences and optimal timing of LRRK2-
41 targeting therapeutics.

52 INTRODUCTION
53 Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a common, progressive neurodegenerative disease, affecting around 1-2% of
54 the population over the age of 65 (1). PD prevalence will likely increase two-fold by the year 2030 (2). In
55 addition, the projected total economic burden will surpass $79 billion by 2037 (3), underscoring the need
56 to reduce PD incidence and delay disease progression. The fact that there are currently no disease-
57 modifying drugs for people with PD indicates that knowledge gaps still need to be closed to identify ways
58 to cure or prevent this disease. Although the etiology of PD is largely unknown, it is thought to involve a
59 complex interaction between various genetic and environmental factors (4). Although typically thought of
60 as a disease limited to the central nervous system (CNS), evidence suggests a crucial and fundamental role
61 of inflammation in both the CNS and the periphery in PD pathogenesis (5). Numerous studies of peripheral
62 blood and cerebrospinal fluid from patients with PD suggest alterations in markers of inflammation and
63 immune cell populations that could initiate or exacerbate neuroinflammation and perpetuate the
64 neurodegenerative process (6-9). Furthermore, a number of disease genes and risk factors have been
65 identified as modulators of immune function in PD (10-12).
66 Pathogenic variants in the gene encoding leucine-rich repeat kinase 2 (LRRK2) account for the majority of
67 familial PD patients (13). LRRK2 is a large, complex protein containing six functional domains, two of
68 which have known enzymatic activities; a ROC domain (GTPase activity) and a MAPKK domain (kinase
69 activity). There are numerous single-nucleotide substitutions in each of these domains that have been shown
70 to be pathogenic (14-21). The second most prevalent mutation, R1441C, located in the GTPase ROC
71 domain, is present in about 46% of familial PD that has an origin in the Basque region in Spain (22) and
72 leads to decreased LRRK2 GTPase hydrolysis and increased LRRK2 protein kinase activity (23, 24).
73 LRRK2 is expressed in innate and adaptive immune cells and its expression is tightly regulated by immune
74 stimulation (9, 25-28). Furthermore, LRRK2 is a member of the receptor interacting protein (RIP) kinase
75 family, which is a group of proteins that detect and respond to cellular stress by regulating cell death and

82 Although research has focused primarily on neuroinflammation in PD over the last two decades, it is
83 becoming increasingly clear that peripheral inflammatory responses may play important roles in PD
84 pathogenesis (37, 38). For example, immune signaling plays a role in the regulation of neurodegeneration
85 in a LRRK2-PD pre-clinical model, with the R1441C mutation altering the type II interferon response in
86 peripheral immune cells (28). Furthermore, neutralization of IL-6 over-production in the periphery of these
87 mice prevents substantia nigra pars compacta (SNpc) dopaminergic (DA) neuron loss in the presence of
88 peripheral lipopolysaccharide (LPS) exposure (39). Importantly, immune dysfunction in the periphery takes
89 primacy over the CNS, as the presence of Lrrk2 mutations in lymphocytes alone is sufficient to induce DA
90 neuron loss in the midbrain of LPS-treated mice (39). Such data suggest a pertinent role of the peripheral
91 immune system in LRRK2-PD, with immune dysfunction driving the development of the disease.
92 As well as a role in immune response, a vast amount of research has focused on the role of LRRK2 at the
93 lysosome. In immune cells, specifically, LRRK2 has been shown to mediate a number of lysosome-

95 LRRK2 has been shown to alter lysosomal function and autophagy in kidneys in an age-dependent, biphasic
96 manner, with enhanced autophagy levels in young Lrrk2-null mice, that are reduced and drop below control
97 levels once the mice reach 20 months of age (42). Despite this intriguing observation, few studies have
98 investigated the effects of LRRK2 mutations on lysosomal processes in the context of aging. Considering
99 age is the greatest risk factor for many neurodegenerative diseases, aging, in particular aging of the immune

102 To close this important gap, we assessed the effects of LRRK2 mutations on immune responses and
103 lysosomal function in peripheral immune cells in young versus old mice. We used R1441C-Lrrk2 mutant
104 knock-in (KI) mice based on previous findings demonstrating an effect of the R1441C/G mutation on
105 peripheral immune responses (28, 39) whilst evading limitations of an overexpression model and
106 investigating the effects of the R1441C mutation in physiologically relevant levels. Peritoneal macrophages
107 (pMacs) were isolated from these mice to examine the effects of the R1441C mutation on macrophage cell
108 function at different ages. pMacs are not a homogenous population of cells, but rather a mix of small and
109 large pMacs (SPMs and LPMs, respectively). LPMs are resident to the peritoneal cavity and are
110 traditionally thought of as anti-inflammatory, phagocytic and responsible for the presentation of exogenous
111 antigens (43). SPMs, on the other hand, are generated from bone-marrow-derived myeloid precursors which
112 migrate to the peritoneal cavity in response to infection, inflammatory stimuli, or thioglycolate, and present
113 a pro-inflammatory functional profile (43). We observed a bi-phasic, age-dependent effect of the R1441C-
114 Lrrk2 KI mice, with increased antigen presentation, production of anti-inflammatory cytokines, lysosomal
115 activity, and pathogen uptake in peripheral macrophages from young (2-3 months) R1441C-Lrrk2 KI mice
116 examined ex vivo. Conversely, peripheral macrophages from aged (18-21 months) R1441C-Lrrk2 KI mice
117 exhibited a diminished capacity to present antigens in the presence of inflammatory insults, decreased
118 lysosomal function and pathogen uptake, with concomitant increases in DNA fragmentation in the presence

120 KI failing to present this age-dependent phenotype. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the age-dependent

129 LRRK2 R1441C mutant immune cells display age-dependent biphasic alteration in antigen
130 presentation
131 LRRK2 protein expression has been shown to increase in immune cells from both murine pre-clinical
132 models and patient cells in response to inflammatory stimuli (9, 27, 28, 44-46). To determine whether this
133 is age-dependent, Lrrk2 protein expression were assessed in pMacs from young and aged mice treated with
134 100U of IFNγ for 18h. In pMacs from young females, IFNγ treatment significantly increased total Lrrk2
135 protein in both B6 and R1441C pMacs (Fig. 1A, B; p < 0.01). Relative to pMacs from young female mice,
136 pMacs from aged females expressed increased total Lrrk2 protein under vehicle stimulation, with aged
137 R1441C pMacs expressing significantly less Lrrk2 protein relative to B6 aged pMacs (p <0.05).
138 Furthermore, pMacs from aged females did not exhibit a significant increase in Lrrk2 protein expression
139 with IFNγ treatment, with aged R1441C pMacs still expressing significantly less Lrrk2 protein relative to
140 B6 aged pMacs in IFNγ-treatment (p<0.05). In pMacs from young male mice, the same observations were
141 made as in the females (Fig. S1A, B). In aged male mice, although a lack of IFNγ-mediated Lrrk2 increase

143 indicate that Lrrk2 protein increase in macrophages as a function of age in both males and females, however,
144 the R1441C mutation limits this increase in a sex-dependent manner.

235 The R1441C Lrrk2 mutation is associated with alterations of monocyte populations and microglia
236 activation in aged female mice

507 DISCUSSION
508 Considering age is the greatest risk factor for many neurodegenerative diseases, aging, in particular aging
509 of the immune system, is the most underappreciated and understudied contributing factor in the
510 neurodegeneration field. Our novel findings reveal a bi-phasic age- and sex-dependent effect of the R1441C
511 mutation in peripheral immune cells, with increased cytokine release, antigen presentation, phagocytosis
512 and lysosomal activity in macrophages from young female R1441C mice, with many of these phenotypes
513 ameliorated by Lrrk2 protein kinase inhibition. Our findings further reveal an age-acquired immune cell
514 exhaustion in macrophages from R1441C females associated with hypophagocytosis, decreased antigen
515 presentation, and reduced lysosomal pan-cathepsin activation and protein degradation, increased pathogen-
516 mediated DNA breakage, and increased PDL1 expression indicative of immune cell exhaustion.
517 Importantly, this immune cell exhaustion phenotype was also observed in monocytes and macrophages
518 from human subjects with PD and harboring the R1441C and the Y1699C mutation. Furthermore, it was

526 of both IL10 and IL6 in circulating plasma from young R1441C mice. It has recently been shown that

529 IL10 is produced by macrophages and is critical in limiting immune-mediated pathology (62), and it was

539 Consistent with these reports, we observed increased E. coli uptake and decreased E. coli-mediated DNA

547 For the first time, we have reported here an age-dependent, bi-phasic effect of LRRK2 on immune cell
548 function, with the R1441C mutation inducing myeloid cell immune cell exhaustion in a sex-dependent
549 manner. The concept of immune cell exhaustion was first described as the clonal deletion of virus-specific
550 CD8+ T-cells that occurs during high-grade chronic infections, and in T-cells is characterized by the
551 stepwise and progressive loss of T-cell functions (68). Regarding exhausted myeloid cells, pathogenic
552 inflammation and immunosuppression, characterized by decreased antigen presentation, hypophagocytosis
553 and altered cytokine release, have been described more recently (48). The immune system is composed of
554 numerous cell types with varying states of activation, and cell-to-cell communication during immune
555 responses is crucial for host defense and resolution of inflammation. The observations made here in innate
556 immune cells, therefore, likely have downstream effects on myeloid cell communication with adaptive
557 immune cell types in the periphery. Indeed, alterations in the adaptive immune system have been reported
558 in PD patients. For example, a meta-analysis of 943 cases of PD identified a reduction in circulating CD4+
559 T cells in PD-patients (69, 70). More specifically, HLA-DR+ T cells and CD45RO+ memory T cells have
560 been shown to be increased in people with PD relative to healthy age- and sex-matched controls, while
561 naive CD4+ T cells are reduced (70-72). CD4+FOXP3+ Treg cells have increased suppressive activity in
562 patients with PD (73). Interestingly, one characteristic of immune cell exhaustion is the dysregulated cross-
563 talk between CD4+ and CD8+ T-cells due to the suppressive action of Tregs (74). Furthermore, Tregs
564 directly inhibit the antigen-presenting function of monocytes and macrophages (75). Further research is
565 required to understand the role of cell-to-cell communication in these myeloid cell phenotypes, and if
566 immune cell exhaustion driven by LRRK2 is a contributing factor.
