In Brief
We conducted an in-depth
proteome analysis of human
substantia nigra tissues from 15
Parkinson’s disease (PD)
patients and 15 healthy control
individuals to uncover
dysregulated pathways in PD.
We identified 10,040 proteins
with 1140 differentially
expressed proteins in the
substantia nigra of PD patients,
discovering mitoribosome
proteins were the most
dysregulated proteins, followed
by ribosome, RNA splicing, and
complement proteins. This study
has discovered that
mitoribosome dysfunction is
potentially involved in the PD
pathogenesis process for the
first time.
Highlights
•
Proteome analysis of Parkinson’s brains identified >10,000 proteins.
•
RNA splicing and complement proteins were upregulated in Parkinson’s brain.
•
Mitoribosome proteins were downregulated in Parkinson’s brain.
