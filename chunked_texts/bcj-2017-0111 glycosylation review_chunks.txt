Role of glycosylation in nucleating protein folding
and stability
Nisha Grandhi Jayaprakash and Avadhesha Surolia
MolecularBiophysicsUnit,IndianInstituteofScience,Bangalore560012,India
Correspondence:AvadheshaSurolia(surolia@mbu.iisc.ernet.in)
Glycosylation constitutes one of the most common, ubiquitous and complex forms of
post-translational modification. It commences with the synthesis of the protein and plays
a significant role in deciding its folded state, oligomerization and thus its function. Recent
studies have demonstrated that N-linked glycans help proteins to fold as the stability and
folding kinetics are altered with the removal of the glycans from them. Several studies
have shown that it alters not only the thermodynamic stability but also the structural fea-
tures of the folded proteins modulating their interactions and functions. Their inhibition
and perturbations have been implicated in diseases from diabetes to degenerative disor-
ders. The intent of this review is to provide insight into the recent advancements in the
general understanding on the aspect of glycosylation driven stability of proteins that is
imperative to their function and finally their role in health and disease states.
Introduction
What is glycosylation?
Glycosylation is the covalent addition of sugar moieties to the protein co-translationally or as a post-
translational modification enzymatically. It is the most frequent, heterogeneous, stereo-chemically
complex modification. The rich repertoire of this modification serves as a profitable mechanism for
augmenting the potential of the genome. A recent review throws light on the multifunctional role of
glycans inorganismal diversity [1].

Conclusion
Recent studies have established the role of glycosylation in a myriad of biological processes like the attainment
of protein structure, oligomerization, protein trafficking, reduction in protein aggregation, modulation of
protein function, ER-QC and in protein homeostasis. They mainly affect properties, such as protein solubility,
polarity and bioavailability, and shield hydrophobic patches and sensitivity to protease enzymes. Studies have
also explored the various cellular functions wherein the glycans playacritical role in cellular growth, differenti-
ation, cell–cell communication, signal transduction, inflammatory and immune response. The impact of glyco-
sylation for a protein is dependent on the location, chemical environment and number of glycosylated sites in
the protein while on the other hand, the size, flexibility, structure and number of branches affect the glycosyla-
tion from the glycan’s perspective. Th
