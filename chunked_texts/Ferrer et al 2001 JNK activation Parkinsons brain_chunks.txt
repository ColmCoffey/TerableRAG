Introduction
Parkinson’s disease (PD) and Dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB) are charac-
terized by the presence of intracytoplasmic inclusions called Lewy bodies
(LBs) localized in neurons of the substantia nigra pars compacta, locus
ceruleus and other selected nuclei of the brain stem, as well as several nuclei
of the basal forebrain and hippocampus. PD and DLB are considered LB
diseases. LBs of cortical type are extended to neurons of the entorhinal
cortex, cerebral isocortex and diencephalon in DLB (Kosaka, 1993; Pollanen
et al., 1993; Forno, 1996; Lennox and Lowe, 1998; Ince et al., 1998; Galvin
et al., 1999). Ultrastructurally, brainstem-type LBs are composed of a dense
core surrounded by radiating thick and thin fibrils. Cortical-type LBs are
composed of loosely arranged fibrils with no distinct dense cores (Duffy and
Tennyson, 1965; Pappolla, 1986; Kuzuhara et al., 1998; Forno et al., 1986). LBs
contain phosphorylated and non-phosphorylated neurofilaments (Bancher
et al., 1989; Hill et al., 1991; Schmidt et al., 1991; Galloway et al., 1992) and
ubiquitin (Kuzuhara et al., 1988; Bancher et al., 1989; Galloway et al., 1992;
Manetto et al., 1988; Lennox et al., 1989). α-synuclein, a cytosolic protein
abundant in presynaptic terminals, is another major component of brainstem
and cortical LBs (Spillantini et al., 1997; Wakabayashi et al., 1997; Baba et al.,
1998; Spillantini et al., 1998; Irizarry et al., 1998; Takeda et al., 1998; Arima et
al., 1998; Trojanowski et al., 1998; Hashimoto and Masliah, 1999). Alzheimer-
type pathology, including senile plaques and neurofibrillary degeneration
with abnormal tau deposition, is a common associated feature in DLB (Ince
et al., 1998; Gómez-Tortosa et al., 2000). LBD with no AD changes is known
as the pure form of DLB, whereas LBD with associated AD changes is
recognized as the common form of DLB (Kosaka, 1993).

kinases (JNKs) and p38 kinases (Cobb and Goldsmith, 1995; Robinson and
Cobb, 1997; Mielke and Herdegen, 2000; Ip and Davis, 1998; Nebreda
and Porras, 2000). Previous studies have shown the involvement of active,

tau deposits in neurons and glial cells in tauopathies (Ferrer et al., 2002).
Finally, strong phosphorylated SAPK/JNK immunoreactivity has been re-
ported in association with phosphorylated tau deposits in neurons and glial
cells in AD and other tauopathies (Ferrer et al., 2002).
The present study examines the expression of ERK, and phosphorylated
MAPK/ERK, p38 and SAPK/JNK in the brains of PD, and pure and common
forms of LBD, in order to gain understanding about the possible involvement
of these kinases in LBs and related α-synuclein deposits in PD and DLB in
comparison with neurofibrillary pathology and related phosphorylated tau
deposits in the common form of LBD.
Material and methods
Human cases
Samples of five patients with PD, nine patients with DLB and five age-matched
controls were obtained at post-mortem. The mean duration of PD was 14 years. The
mean duration of DLB was 8.6 years. The causes of death were similar in control
and neurodegenerative cases. Patients with long agonal states were not included in this
series. The post-mortem delay was between 2h and 7h in control and diseased
brains.
