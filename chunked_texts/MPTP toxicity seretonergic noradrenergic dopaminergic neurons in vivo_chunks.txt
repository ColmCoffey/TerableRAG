MPP + (1-methyl-4-phenylpyridine) is a neurotoxin to dopamine-,
norepinephrine- and serotonin-containing neurons

1-Methyl-4-phenylpyridine (MPP +) is an oxidative metabolite of 1-methyl-4-phenyl-l,2,3,6-tetrahydroxypyridine
(MPTP). MPP ÷ produced local cell death when injected directly into substantia nigra compacta, locus coeruleus, or
dorsal and median raphe nuclei of rats. Corresponding significant decreases in dopamine, serotonin and norepineph-
fine levels were observed in the terminal areas. These observations indicate that MPP + is a non-selective neurotoxin
which causes lesions not only in dopaminergic neurons but also in noradrenergic and serotonergic systems following
intracranial administration. Selective lesioning of these monoaminergic systems could only be achieved by a
stereotaxic injection of MPP + into specific brain regions containing monoamine neurons.

Serotonin and dopamine in both areas on the

serotonin content of cingulate cortex and nucleus

ascending dopaminergic or serotonergic fibers in
the vicinity of the locus coeruleus.
The cingulate cortex and olfactory tubercle re-
ceive serotonin innervation from the median raphe
nucleus, whereas the striatum receives its innerva-
tion from the dorsal raphe nucleus (Parent et al.,
1981). Serotonin levels were decreased by 70-80%
in all of these terminal areas following the MPP ÷
infusion into both dorsal and median raphe nuclei
(table 3). It would appear to be difficult to lesion
all of the serotonergic cell bodies since they are
distributed widely throughout the brain-stem
(Singhaniyom, 1984). In the raphe-lesioned rats,
norepinephrine and dopamine levels were not dif-
ferent from the control, which probably reflects
the fact that these ascending catecholamine bun-
dles do not pass through the raphe nucleus.
