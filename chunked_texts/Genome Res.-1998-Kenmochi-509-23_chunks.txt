We mapped 75 genes that collectively encode >90% of the proteins found in human ribosomes. Because
localization of ribosomal protein genes (rp genes) is complicated by the existence of processed pseudogenes,
multiple strategies were devised to identify PCR-detectable sequence-tagged sites (STSs) at introns. In some cases
we exploited specific, pre-existing information about the intron/exon structure of a given human rp gene or its
homolog in another vertebrate. When such information was unavailable, selection of PCR primer pairs was
guided by general insights gleaned from analysis of all mammalian rp genes whose intron/exon structures have
been published. For many genes, PCR amplification of introns was facilitated by use of YAC pool DNAs rather
than total human genomic DNA as templates. We then assigned the rp gene STSs to individual human
chromosomes by typing human–rodent hybrid cell lines. The genes were placed more precisely on the physical
map of the human genome by typing of radiation hybrids or screening YAC libraries. Fifty-one previously
unmapped rp genes were localized, and 24 previously reported rp gene localizations were confirmed, refined, or
corrected. Though functionally related and coordinately expressed, the 75 mapped genes are widely dispersed:
Both sex chromosomes and at least 20 of the 22 autosomes carry one or more rp genes. Chromosome 19,
